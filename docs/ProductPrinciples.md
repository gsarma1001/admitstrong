# AdmitStrong Product Principles

## Purpose

These principles guide product, design, engineering, content, and AI decisions.

---

## 1. Every Student Deserves a Clear Path Forward

Guidance should be understandable and accessible regardless of income,
background, school resources, or personal networks.

---

## 2. Always Clarify the Next Best Step

The product should help the student understand:

- what to do,
- why it matters,
- how to begin,
- and when to revisit it.

---

## 3. Reduce Anxiety

Do not use fear, shame, comparison, or manipulative urgency.

Present complex journeys as manageable steps.

---

## 4. Explain Recommendations

Important recommendations should identify:

- the relevant student context,
- the goal being supported,
- important assumptions,
- and factual sources when applicable.

Avoid unexplained scores.

---

## 5. Personalize Meaningfully

Personalization should use relevant differences in:

- academics,
- interests,
- activities,
- goals,
- preferences,
- resources,
- constraints,
- and stage of development.

Do not personalize merely by inserting a student's name.

---

## 6. Build Momentum

Prefer small, achievable, high-value actions over large vague assignments.

Celebrate meaningful completion without turning the experience into a game of
collecting points.

---

## 7. Design for the Whole Student

Support exploration, growth, contribution, well-being, and informed choice—not
only admission outcomes.

---

## 8. Preserve Student Agency

The student should be able to:

- understand recommendations,
- edit profile information,
- question assumptions,
- decline or defer actions,
- and make the final decision.

---

## 9. Support Families Without Encouraging Micromanagement

Give parents useful context and appropriate visibility while preserving the
student's ownership of the journey.

---

## 10. Use AI Responsibly

AI should organize, explain, suggest, and help students think.

It should not:

- guarantee admission,
- invent facts,
- hide uncertainty,
- make high-stakes decisions autonomously,
- or replace qualified human support.

---

## 11. Protect Trust

Collect only necessary information.

Be honest about:

- what is known,
- what is estimated,
- what is generated,
- where data came from,
- and when human review is appropriate.

---

## 12. Progress Over Perfection

A helpful next action today is more valuable than a theoretically complete plan
that overwhelms the student.
