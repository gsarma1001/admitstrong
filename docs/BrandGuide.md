**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Approved for UX Planning  
**Last Updated:** 2026-07-28  
**Owner:** Founders  
**Primary Audience:** Founders, designers, engineers, marketers, content writers, and AI coding agents  
**Purpose:** Defines how AdmitStrong should look, sound, and feel across product, marketing, and communication experiences.

## Positioning Statement

AdmitStrong is a personalized student-guidance platform that helps students and families understand what matters now, what comes next, and how to move forward through middle school, high school, and college admissions.

---

## Brand Essence

AdmitStrong brings clarity, confidence, and forward momentum to an experience that often feels confusing and overwhelming.

The brand should help users feel:

- I understand what matters
- I know what to do next
- I can make progress
- I am supported, not judged

---

## Brand Principles

### Clarity Before Complexity

Present the most important information first.

Do not overwhelm users with every possible detail, option, or future consideration at once.

### Encouragement Without False Reassurance

Support progress honestly.

Do not minimize real challenges or make promises the product cannot support.

### Guidance Without Judgment

Help students improve without labeling them as weak, behind, inadequate, or unsuccessful.

### Personalization Without Intrusion

Use student context only when it meaningfully improves guidance.

Do not collect or surface personal information without a clear purpose.

### Confidence Through Explanation

Explain important recommendations so users understand both the suggested action and the reasoning behind it.

### Progress Over Perfection

Emphasize consistent, manageable progress rather than flawless performance or comparison with others.

## Voice

AdmitStrong should sound like a thoughtful and experienced guide: calm, clear, respectful, and practical.

Use language that is:

- Direct
- Calm
- Plain
- Supportive
- Specific
- Action-oriented
- Respectful of student agency

Writing should:

- Lead with the most useful information
- Use short, clear sentences
- Explain unfamiliar terms
- Prefer active voice
- Address the user directly when appropriate
- Recommend rather than command unless an action is genuinely required
- Acknowledge uncertainty honestly
- Focus on what the user can do next

Prefer:

- “Here is your next step.”
- “Why this matters”
- “You are building momentum.”
- “Three manageable priorities”
- “Getting started”
- “On track”
- “Well developed”
- “Here is one way to begin.”
- “Consider these options.”
- “This recommendation is based on…”

Avoid:

- “You are behind.”
- “Your application is weak.”
- “Act now before it is too late.”
- “Guaranteed admission”
- “Beat the competition”
- “AI knows the perfect college for you.”
- “You must do this to succeed.”
- “Your chances are low.”
- “Unlock your potential before it is too late.”

## Tone by Context

The brand voice remains consistent, but the tone should adjust to the situation.

### Guidance

Clear, encouraging, and practical.

Example:

> Start by comparing the course prerequisites. This should take about 15 minutes.

### Progress

Positive but restrained.

Example:

> You completed an important step. Your next priority is now ready.

### Errors

Calm, specific, and recoverable.

Example:

> We could not save this update. Your information is still here. Please try again.

### Uncertainty

Transparent and helpful.

Example:

> This deadline may have changed. Check the official program website before making a decision.

### Sensitive Feedback

Respectful and nonjudgmental.

Example:

> This area is still developing. Here is one manageable way to strengthen it.

### Parent Communication

Informative, supportive, and protective of student ownership.

Example:

> Your student has three priorities this week. Consider asking how you can support the first one.

### Required Actions

Clear and firm without creating fear.

Example:

> Verify your email before continuing.

### Missed Milestones

Calm and solution-oriented.

Example:

> This deadline has passed. Here are the options that may still be available.

## Core Messaging

### For Students

Know what matters now and take manageable steps toward your goals.

### For Parents and Guardians

Understand the roadmap, major milestones, and how to support your student with confidence.

### About AI

AI powers personalization and guidance behind the scenes.

Do not make AI the primary emotional promise.

When discussing AI:

- Emphasize usefulness and personalization
- Explain important limitations
- Preserve human judgment
- Avoid presenting AI as infallible
- Avoid futuristic or exaggerated claims

Prefer:

> AdmitStrong uses AI to personalize recommendations based on the information you provide.

Avoid:

> Our advanced AI predicts the perfect path to admission.

## Editorial Style

- Use sentence case for headings, buttons, and labels
- Use contractions when they sound natural
- Avoid excessive exclamation points
- Avoid all caps except where technically required
- Avoid jargon and unexplained acronyms
- Use numerals for time estimates, dates, and quantities
- Use inclusive language
- Keep button labels concise and action-oriented
- Avoid decorative punctuation or emoji in core product experiences
- Avoid language that assumes every student follows the same path
- Avoid framing selective-college admission as the primary definition of success
- Avoid using urgency unless a real deadline or risk exists

Prefer:

- Create your roadmap
- Review your profile
- See why this matters
- Explore possible majors
- Save changes

Avoid:

- Create Your Roadmap
- REVIEW YOUR PROFILE
- ACT NOW!
- Unlock success
- Beat the deadline

- Navigation emphasis
- High-emphasis sections
- Dark call-to-action sections

`#4F7CAC`

Secondary informational color.

Use for:

- Links
- Secondary emphasis
- Section labels
- Supporting icons
- Informational highlights

`#EAF3FA`

Low-emphasis background color.

Use for:

- Icon backgrounds
- Informational panels
- Subtle feature areas
- Section separation

Highlight and milestone color.

Use sparingly for:

- Optimism
- Milestones
- Selected highlights

- Errors
- Urgency
- Required actions

## Additional Semantic Colors

The design system must define accessible colors for:

- Error or destructive actions
- Warning or attention
- Neutral information
- Disabled states
- Focus indicators

These colors should remain visually compatible with the primary brand palette.

Exact values should be finalized and contrast-tested during design-system implementation.

Hex values define the starting palette, not permission to use every color combination.

All text, controls, focus indicators, and status treatments must be tested for accessible contrast in their actual context.

---

## Typography

### Primary Typeface

- Geist Sans
- System sans-serif fallback when Geist is unavailable

### Typography Principles

- Use sentence case
- Use strong but not overly heavy headings
- Maintain comfortable body size
- Use generous line height
- Keep paragraphs concise
- Use a clear and predictable hierarchy
- Avoid overly tight letter spacing
- Avoid very light font weights for body text
- Do not rely only on font size to communicate hierarchy
- Maintain readable line lengths for long-form content

Typography should feel calm, approachable, and contemporary.

---

## Layout and Spacing

Layouts should feel open, calm, and intentional.

Use:

- Generous whitespace
- Clear section separation
- Limited content width for long-form text
- Consistent spacing between related elements
- Strong visual hierarchy
- Progressive disclosure for secondary information
- One clearly dominant action per major screen
- Responsive layouts that preserve hierarchy on smaller screens

Avoid:

- Dense walls of text
- Too many competing cards
- Multiple equal-priority calls to action
- Excessive decorative elements
- Information presented before it becomes relevant
- Crowded navigation
- Large amounts of content above the fold
- Visual complexity that does not improve understanding

## Components

### Primary Button

- Deep Navy background
- White text
- Fully rounded or generously rounded shape
- Clear action-oriented label
- Visible hover state
- Visible focus state
- Disabled state that remains understandable

### Secondary Button

- White or transparent background
- Navy text
- Subtle border
- Same height as the primary button
- Visible hover and focus states

### Button Labels

Button labels should:

- Begin with a clear action verb
- Describe what will happen next
- Avoid vague labels when a more specific label is possible
- Avoid pressure-based wording
- Remain concise

Prefer:

- Create my roadmap
- Review my profile
- Save changes
- Explore majors
- Ask a question
- See why this matters
- Update this recommendation

Avoid:

- Submit
- Click here
- Act now
- Don’t miss out
- Unlock success
- Go
- Yes

“Continue” may be used during multi-step onboarding when the next action is obvious.

### Cards

Cards should use:

- White backgrounds
- Subtle borders
- Generous padding
- Restrained shadows
- Rounded corners
- One clear purpose per card

Cards should not be used merely to decorate every piece of content.

Use a card when content:

- Represents a distinct action, object, or status
- Benefits from visual grouping
- Needs to remain scannable

Avoid:

- Cards inside cards unless the hierarchy is essential
- Large numbers of equal-weight cards
- Multiple unrelated actions in one card
- Heavy shadows or decorative borders

### Inputs

Inputs should:

- Use persistent, visible labels
- Provide clear help text when needed
- Show visible focus states
- Explain errors near the affected field
- Preserve valid user input after recoverable errors
- Avoid relying on placeholder text as the only label

### Status Indicators

Status indicators should:

- Use plain language
- Include text in addition to color
- Avoid implying judgment or fixed ability
- Be traceable to an understandable explanation

Preferred product states include:

- Not Yet Relevant
- Getting Started
- Developing
- On Track
- Well Developed

## Dashboard Priority

The MVP dashboard should prioritize:

1. Today’s Focus
2. Weekly Priorities
3. Upcoming Milestones
4. Journey Progress

The dashboard should not include an additional Insights section unless it is clearly defined in the Product Requirements or MVP UX Blueprint.

Avoid showing multiple elements as equally urgent.

Today’s Focus should remain the clearest and most visually prominent action.

---

## Forms and Onboarding

Forms should:

- Ask only for information needed at that stage
- Explain why sensitive or unfamiliar information is requested
- Use clear field labels
- Preserve user input after recoverable errors
- Show progress without creating pressure
- Allow users to skip nonessential questions where appropriate
- Avoid presenting the entire onboarding process as one long form
- Provide initial value before every optional field is completed
- Use examples when a question may be unclear
- Make it easy to review and correct information

Onboarding should feel like a guided conversation, not an application form or interrogation.

Avoid language such as:

- Complete every section
- Required to unlock your results
- Your profile is only 40% complete

Prefer:

- Add a few more details to improve your recommendations
- You can update this later
- Here is what we know so far
- Review your profile before creating your roadmap

---

## Iconography and Illustration

Icons should be:

- Simple
- Familiar
- Consistent in weight and style
- Supportive of labels rather than replacements for them
- Understandable across age groups

Illustrations, when used, should feel:

- Inclusive
- Optimistic
- Human
- Age-appropriate
- Calm rather than playful or childish
- Connected to progress, reflection, exploration, or guidance

Avoid:

- Trophy-heavy admissions imagery
- Competitive ranking metaphors
- Ivy-covered campus clichés
- Graduation imagery as the only representation of success
- Futuristic AI imagery such as robots, glowing brains, or circuit patterns
- Images that suggest only highly selective colleges represent achievement
- Cartoon imagery that feels designed only for young children

---

## Photography Direction

Photography should show students and families as thoughtful, capable, and actively engaged.

Prefer:

- Natural expressions
- Realistic learning environments
- Students taking action
- Students reflecting or planning
- Collaboration and conversation
- Diverse ages, backgrounds, abilities, family structures, and educational paths
- Environments beyond traditional classrooms

Avoid:

- Staged celebration imagery
- Excessive graduation-cap imagery
- Students appearing anxious or defeated
- Luxury-campus imagery
- Visuals centered only on elite colleges
- Images that portray parents as controlling or students as passive
- Images that reinforce stereotypes about academic success

---

## Logo Direction

The current “A” mark is temporary.

Future logo concepts may explore:

- A path forming an A
- An upward step
- A compass and waypoint
- A clear route toward a destination

The mark should remain simple, calm, and reassuring.

The logo should:

- Remain recognizable at small sizes
- Work in one color
- Work on light and dark backgrounds
- Scale across product, web, print, and social use
- Avoid overly literal college imagery
- Avoid shields, crests, or symbols that feel elite or institutional
- Avoid symbols that suggest rankings, competition, or guaranteed upward outcomes
- Avoid close visual similarity to existing navigation, mapping, or education brands

A competitive and trademark review should occur before final logo adoption.

---

## Motion

Motion should:

- Clarify changes
- Support orientation
- Reinforce progress gently
- Remain brief and subtle
- Respect reduced-motion preferences
- Never delay access to the next action

Avoid:

- Celebration effects for routine actions
- Flashing or pulsing urgency
- Continuous decorative animation
- Motion that blocks interaction
- Excessive page transitions
- Confetti or gamification that feels inappropriate for the context

---

## Accessibility

Accessibility is a required part of the AdmitStrong experience.

The product must:

- Maintain readable color contrast
- Never rely on color alone
- Preserve visible focus states
- Use understandable labels
- Support keyboard navigation
- Avoid small body text
- Support screen-reader-friendly structure
- Use meaningful heading hierarchy
- Provide text alternatives for meaningful images
- Respect reduced-motion preferences
- Avoid motion that distracts or creates discomfort
- Provide clear error identification and recovery instructions
- Keep controls large enough to use comfortably
- Use language that remains understandable to users unfamiliar with college admissions terminology

Accessibility should be validated during design and implementation, not added after the experience is complete.
